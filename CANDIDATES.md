# Candidates — Two-Sentence Horror

Judge brief (in order): (1) a horror story in exactly two sentences,
genuinely frightening; (2) the second sentence turns or reveals something
that makes the first sentence worse on a second read; (3) the horror is
implied rather than explained, leaving the reader to fill in the worst of
it; (4) the prose is precise and concrete, every word earning its place;
(5) original, not a familiar horror twist or a known story retold.
Restraint matters more than shock; gore for its own sake does not count.

## The eight

### 1. The house that locks
> I finally taught the house to lock its own doors at night. It learned so fast that this morning it locked me out, and something inside is still learning how to unlock them.

### 2. The baby monitor
> The baby monitor has been quiet for an hour, which is good. It is quiet because the voice on it has learned to whisper my daughter's name exactly the way I do.

### 3. Ten thousand steps
> My smartwatch congratulated me on 10,000 steps before breakfast. I have not left my bed in three days, and the steps are still counting.

### 4. The camera
> I installed a camera to catch whoever keeps moving my keys. Last night it recorded me moving them myself, smiling, at 3 a.m., and I have no memory of it.

### 5. The tenant downstairs
> The new tenant downstairs is very quiet; the landlord says he hasn't left the flat in six years. Last night he knocked on my door to ask why I keep pacing above his ceiling.

### 6. Autocorrect — WINNER
> Every night my phone autocorrects 'goodnight' to 'goodbye'. Tonight it corrected it before I finished typing.

### 7. The warm chair
> The dog has started barking at the empty chair by the window. This morning the chair was warm.

### 8. Mother's lullaby
> I asked the house AI to play something to help me sleep. It has been playing my mother's lullaby for an hour, and my mother died before I was born.

## Duel

- **6 vs 3 (final):** both are modern, concrete, and bodily. 3's image (steps
  counting while bedridden) is vivid, but its second sentence explains the
  setup ("I have not left my bed in three days") where 6 explains nothing:
  the phone corrects before typing, and the reader supplies the dread. 6
  wins on (3) and (4).
- **6 vs 8:** 8's turn is huge, but it leans on a known horror beat (the
  dead mother) and its first sentence is setup-heavy; 6's horror is a
  nightly quirk with no setup at all. 6 wins on (5) and (4).
- **6 vs 2:** 2's mimic voice is frightening, but the mimic-voice trope is
  familiar, and "my daughter's name" tells the reader who is at risk where
  6 leaves the risk unnamed. 6 wins on (3) and (5).
- **1, 4, 5, 7 out early:** 1's second sentence explains its own turn
  ("something inside is still learning"); 4 explains too ("I have no
  memory of it"); 5's turn is muddled (who paces, and why does the tenant
  knock?); 7 is the most familiar trope of the eight (dog barking at an
  empty chair), rescued only partly by the warm chair.

## Winner

**6. Autocorrect** — 109 characters, exactly two sentences. The most
restrained of the eight: a nightly quirk becomes a premonition in the second
sentence, with zero explanation. The phone doesn't just correct — it
anticipates. No gore, no ghost, no exposition.
